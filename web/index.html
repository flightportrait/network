Join the network.

The aircraft on this map were heard by receivers run by the people who feed the network. A receiver is a Raspberry Pi with a small antenna; once set up, it shares what it hears automatically. Running one adds coverage where you live.

Your station identity

There is no signup: this UUID is your station's identity. It is already in the feeder line and the brief below, and this page uses it to spot your station when it connects. A new UUID is a new station.

Already have a receiver.

Add this line to your feeder configuration, next to the networks you already feed:

adsb,feed.flightportrait.com,30004,beast_reduce_plus_out,uuid=YOUR-UUID

Build your own receiver.

You will need a Raspberry Pi (any model from 2 to 5) with a power supply and a microSD card, plus an RTL-SDR USB dongle with an antenna, about US$40. The receiver tracks aircraft within 200 to 400 km, depending on the antenna's view of the sky.

To set it up, copy the brief below and paste it into Claude Code, Codex, or any AI assistant that can use your computer, then answer its questions. It does everything that can be done from your machine and walks you through the rest. Or follow the steps by hand.

The steps by hand.
  1. Buy a Raspberry Pi 4 (any memory size, new or used, with power supply and a microSD card; the link lists official resellers by country) and the RTL-SDR Blog V4 dongle with antenna kit (about US$40, ships worldwide). Any Raspberry Pi 2 through 5 you already own works too.
  2. Flash the adsb.im feeder image onto the microSD card with Raspberry Pi Imager. It is a free, open image made for exactly this.
  3. Plug antenna into dongle, dongle into Pi, Pi into power, near a window. Reception depends on how much sky the antenna can see.
  4. On first boot the Pi starts its own WiFi hotspot. Join it from your phone and it walks you through connecting to your home network.
  5. On its setup page, under custom aggregators, enter host feed.flightportrait.com, port 30004, and your UUID from above.
